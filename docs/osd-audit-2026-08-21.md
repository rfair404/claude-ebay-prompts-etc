# Why OSD was confidently wrong — the question #21 said to answer first

*2026-08-21. Answers the blocking question in issue #21 before any part of the
decision-record refactor starts.*

## The question

> Why was OSD confidently wrong on five `paul-fredrick` frames — 270 at
> confidence up to 12.2, recognised script, all corroborating, all wrong? If the
> detector's angle convention is composed with EXIF incorrectly for some frames,
> this refactor would not touch it, and it is the one that quietly ships sideways
> photos headless.

## The answer, in one line

The angle convention is fine and EXIF is not double-applied. **OSD was being
believed roughly three points of confidence below where it is worth believing** —
at the old floor it agreed with the operator 49% of the time, on the one signal
this module calls objective.

## What was ruled out, and how

**The convention is correct.** Tested directly rather than reasoned about: a
synthetic page of text was rotated to all four turns and read back.

| true rotation | OSD said | convention expects | |
|---|---|---|---|
| 0° | 0 | 0 | ✅ |
| 90° | 270 | 270 | ✅ |
| 180° | 180 | 180 | ✅ |
| 270° | 90 | 90 | ✅ |

Tesseract's `rotate` is degrees clockwise to apply to make the image upright,
which is exactly what `orientation.py` documents and what `rotate_bgr` does.

**EXIF is not applied twice.** `_load_bgr` goes through `Image.open().convert()`,
which does not transpose; the bake is the explicit `rotate_bgr(bgr, EXIF_ROT[tag])`
in `run_check`, and OSD runs on the result, so it reports the subject half only.
That composition is right.

**The five frames were not all wrong.** On `IMG_20260730_225104128.jpg` the
stored pixels are already upright, EXIF says 90, and OSD's 270 correctly cancels
it — `applied: 0`, which is right. That is the two-rotation model working: the
catalog was laid on the bedspread turned 90° from the way the cover was shot, so
the subject half genuinely differs between frames of one session.

That last point matters on its own, because `prompts/prep.md` still asserts
*"six spreads photographed in one session cannot correctly differ by 90°."*
**On this shoot they did**, and that heuristic is part of why a correct OSD
reading was read as a wrong one.

The frame that *was* wrong is the redacted mailer: OSD 90 at orientation
confidence 12.29 but **script confidence 0.6**, and the upright is 0°.

## What the corpus says

`tools/osd_audit.py` scores every OSD reading in `inventory/` against the human
look recorded beside it. A frame carrying both an `osd_angle` and a
`vision_angle` is a labelled example, because the recorded look outranks OSD by
policy — a disagreement is a correction, not a tie.

**73 labelled frames. OSD agreed with the operator 49% of the time.**

The failure is not spread evenly, which is what makes it fixable:

| orientation confidence | n | agreement |
|---|---|---|
| 0.0 – 2.0 | 14 | 21% |
| **2.0 – 3.0** | **17** | **12%** |
| 3.0 – 4.0 | 11 | 45% |
| 4.0 – 6.0 | 10 | 80% |
| 6.0 + | 21 | 86% |

The old floor of 1.5 admitted the 2–3 band, which is the largest bad bucket in
the corpus and performs **worse than chance**.

Script confidence — the guard added after the cast-iron key — separates far less
well (26% / 48% / 56% / 54% / 82%) and does not rescue the low-confidence
readings. It stays, because it catches a different failure, but it was never the
knob that mattered here.

## The change

`OSD_MIN_CONF: 1.5 → 4.0`

| floor | answers kept | agreement | wrong answers taken |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1.5 (old) | 100% | 49% | 37 |
| 3.0 | 58% | 74% | 11 |
| **4.0 (new)** | **42%** | **84%** | **5** |
| 6.0 | 29% | 86% | 3 |

4.0 is the knee. It gives up 58% of the readings to remove 32 of the 37 wrong
ones, and giving them up is cheap: **a frame with no OSD answer is not guessed
at.** It becomes an ASK and goes to the operator on the rotation sheet, which
the orientation stage is built around anyway. A wrong answer is the expensive
outcome — it is applied silently and ships a sideways photo in a headless run.

## The confound, stated plainly

A recorded look is not perfect ground truth. Issue #21 documents a
relative-vs-absolute `--rotate` bug that moved some frames more than once, so a
few of these `vision_angle`s are themselves corrupted. Read the 49% as
approximate.

That noise cannot manufacture the monotonic climb from 21% to 86% across the
confidence bands, which is the part the threshold rests on.

## What this means for #21

The refactor is still worth doing, and this was not one of the things it would
have fixed — which is exactly why the issue asked first. Two knock-on notes:

- **The decision record should carry the confidence, not just the angle.** Every
  number in this document came from `osd_conf` and `osd_note` happening to be
  persisted. That was luck; make it structural.
- **`prompts/prep.md`'s same-session agreement heuristic needs revising.** It is
  stated as a rule and it is false for `paul-fredrick`.

## Reproduce

```bash
python tools/osd_audit.py --bands --list-wrong
```
