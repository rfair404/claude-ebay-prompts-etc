─── RESEARCH NOTES — Item 2 ───

  Pottery Barn and similar decorator brands produce visually similar
  reproductions of Victorian parlor lamps; the weight + materials
  test is the fastest decisive check. Modern repros are
  significantly lighter than real cast bronze or brass.

Listing restrictions & legal issues:
  Older lamps with original wiring may need disclosure / re-wiring
  notice. Some states/buyers care about lead-paint history on
  vintage painted finishes. Standard "sold as-is, decorative use"
  language recommended.

Known scams & buyer issues:
  "Lamp doesn't work" returns are common for vintage lamps.
  Mitigation: test wiring before listing, photograph the lit lamp,
  and disclose any wiring concerns. Shades damaged in transit also
  common — pack shades separately.

  - Total height with and without shade
  - Pair matched (sold together) vs. non-identical

─── PRICE BRACKET SUMMARY (Item 4) ───
  Scenario 1 (authentic 19th C. Rococo Revival):   $3,000–$8,000+
  Scenario 2 (late Victorian quality repro):       $800–$2,500
  Scenario 3 (mid-20th C. revival):                $400–$1,000
  Scenario 4 (modern decorator repro):             $200–$600

  Verification BEFORE buying — in order:
    1. Marble underside inspection — authentic 19th C. marble shows
       chisel marks and slight irregularity; modern is uniformly
       machine-cut.
    2. Carving inspection on the BACK of carved elements — hand-
       carved shows tool marks; machine-carved is uniform.
    3. Joinery: dovetailed drawers + mortise-and-tenon = 19th C.;
       dowel + screw = 20th C. revival.
    4. Finish: original shellac / varnish (yellowed, sometimes crazed)
       vs. modern polyurethane.
    5. Wear pattern: authentic age shows uneven wear at high-touch
       points; uniform finish = refinish or modern construction.


─── RESEARCH NOTES — Item 4 ───

Authenticity & fakes:
  Mid-20th C. Victorian revival reproductions are common and
  superficially similar to 19th C. originals. The marble underside
  + joinery inspection are the decisive checks. Authentic 19th C.
  pieces also typically show evidence of repairs / restorations
  over their lifetime — perfect-condition pieces should be regarded
  with healthy skepticism.

Listing restrictions & legal issues:
  No regulatory restrictions, BUT:
  - SIZE/WEIGHT: marble top alone may be 50+ lb; full table 100+ lb.
    FREIGHT REQUIRED for any meaningful distance.
  - Marble top often ships separately from base to reduce damage
    risk and freight cost.
  - International shipping rarely viable due to size and weight.

Known scams & buyer issues:
  - "Marble cracked in transit" is the dominant dispute pattern
    for marble-top furniture. Pack with EXTREME care or ship
    marble separately, double-crated.
  - "Doesn't match the photos" returns common — photograph at
    multiple angles in good light, include scale references.
  - Estate sale / auction provenance documentation adds value
    on authentic 19th C. pieces.

Authentication services available:
  Specialty antique furniture appraisers (per-piece fee) for
  high-value attribution decisions. ASA-credentialed appraisers
  for pieces likely above $3,000.

Disclosure recommendations:
  - EXACT dimensions (height × width × depth, marble top
    measurements separately)
  - Total weight (marble alone + base alone)
  - Local pickup only OR freight cost estimate per region
  - Marble condition: any chips, cracks, stains, repairs
  - Wood base condition: any joint separation, lifting veneer,
    finish issues, lost carving details
  - Any drawer / hidden compartment description
  - Original vs. refinished surface
  - Maker plaque / stamps photographed if present
