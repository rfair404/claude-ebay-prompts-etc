Scenario bracket:
  Scenarios (best → worst):
    1. Authentic 19th C. American or French Rococo Revival console
       (c. 1860–1890), original marble, original carving, original
       finish: premium antique tier.
    2. Late-19th to early-20th C. quality reproduction (Renaissance
       Revival or late Victorian commercial production): solid mid-tier.
    3. Mid-20th C. Victorian revival reproduction (1950s–70s decorator
       furniture, machine-carved): modest tier.
    4. Modern decorator reproduction (2000s+ import or factory
       production, marble veneer or composite top): floor tier.
  How to distinguish:
    - Marble inspection: authentic 19th C. marble has visible chisel
      marks on the underside; modern marble has uniform machine-cut
      edges. Color depth and veining patterns also differ.
    - Carving: hand-carved details are crisper and slightly irregular,
      with visible chisel/gouge marks on backs of carved elements;
      machine-carved details are uniform and symmetrical.
    - Joinery: dovetailed drawer construction + mortise-and-tenon
      framing = 19th C.; dowel + screw = 20th C. revival;
      staple + dowel + particleboard = modern.
    - Finish: original shellac/varnish (yellowed, possibly crazed) vs.
      modern polyurethane / paint.
    - Wear pattern: authentic age shows uneven wear at high-touch
      points; refinished antiques and modern repros show uniform
      finish.
  Price-tier swing: very significant.
