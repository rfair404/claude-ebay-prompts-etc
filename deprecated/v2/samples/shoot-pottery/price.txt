─── RESEARCH NOTES — Item 2 ───

Authenticity & fakes:
  Modern decorative reproductions of antique stoneware crocks are a
  major segment of the home decor market (Magnolia / Joanna Gaines /
  Hearth & Hand product lines especially). The visual difference is
  subtle but specific: real salt-glaze has orange-peel surface texture
  under raking light; modern repros are uniformly smooth or have a
  matte finish meant to imitate age. Weight is also a quick tell —
  antique stoneware is dense; modern repros are lighter.

Listing restrictions & legal issues:
  No restrictions. Standard antique stoneware. Lead-glaze concerns
  generally do not apply to authentic American salt-glazed stoneware
  (the salt-glaze process does not use lead) — however, buyers
  occasionally ask about food-safety; recommend disclosing "decorative
  use only, not tested for food contact."
  Shipping: heavy item; calculate weight carefully. Domestic only
  recommended for affordable shipping.

Known scams & buyer issues:
  - "Hairline crack not disclosed" returns — common dispute. Inspect
    rim, body, and foot under bright light pre-sale. A bright pinging
    sound when flicked = sound; dull thud = hidden crack.
  - Buyers sometimes return claiming pieces are "modern repro" when
    they're not, expecting refund + keeping the item. Photograph any
    maker marks and kiss marks pre-sale as documentation.

Authentication services available:
  N/A through eBay; specialty stoneware dealers (American Stoneware
  Collectors LLC, Doc's Crocks, similar) will sometimes authenticate
  for a fee on high-value pieces.

Disclosure recommendations:
  - Height, diameter, weight (essential for shipping)
  - Any chips on rim, foot, or handle attachment (the single most
    common dispute trigger)
  - Hairline cracks visible under light, including interior
  - Maker mark photographed clearly if present (or explicitly noted
    as unmarked)
  - "Decorative use, not tested for food contact" — standard
    disclosure protects against food-safety claims.
