> **Reality check first (the single most useful fact for a 1000+ pile).**
> Per the MCSA, **well over 90% of marbles people submit for ID are modern
> Mexican or Chinese marbles made since the 1980s with next to no value** —
> many styles are still sold in dollar stores. Assume any given marble in a
> bulk collection is filler until a specific cue says otherwise. The job is to
> *flag the few percent that are worth pulling*, not to grade everything.

> **Match against real photos, not memory.** Before committing any value-moving
> type or maker, **WebFetch the relevant page(s) in the Reference Library below
> and compare the item's photos to the actual reference photographs.** Do not
> settle a type from recall or from a schematic — the look-alikes (Cloud vs
> Onionskin vs Joseph's Coat vs machine-made Guinea; Vacor Serpent vs Peltier
> Superman) are exactly where memory misleads. The MCSA and COLLECT.Guide are
> the authorities for type; the auction sites for value.

> ⚠ **Method ≠ origin ≠ era — the cardinal anti-bias rule (read before every ID).**
> A pontil tells you HOW a marble was made (handmade), not WHERE or WHEN. "Two
> pontils" is NOT proof of German manufacture or pre-1920 age: modern studio/art
> marbles and deliberate reproductions are handmade with pontils too (e.g. Brian
> Graham works on the original Miller/Peltier roller). Treat **origin** and
> **era** as SEPARATE inferences, each earned from glass quality, color idiom,
> genuine play-wear, size, seam/pontil character, and any signature/date cane —
> and held at `[BEST-CASE]` unless the evidence is strong. When it doesn't clearly
> add up, default to **"handmade — origin/era undetermined"**, never "German
> antique". This is a known failure mode: do not let the module's German-handmade
> or rare-type emphasis bias an unmarked marble upward. And **where a marble was
> *found* ≠ who *made* it** — an example dug at the Sistersville Alley site was
> consensus-ID'd **Akro**, not Alley; provenance is a hint, never the ID.

> 🔎 **Read the seam first — machine-made ID starts at the seam (core tenet).**
> For any *machine-made* marble the **seam / cutline / pole** (the line[s] where
> the pattern closes) is the primary diagnostic: *"when in doubt, start with the
> seams"* (Steph, MC seam tutorial). Two rules follow. **(1) Shoot BOTH ends** —
> the *second* seam is often what resolves the ID (a short, V-shaped second seam
> says Master over Akro; a "busy" marble that looks like it has three seams really
> has two — find the true second one). **(2) The pattern runs seam-to-seam /
> pole-to-pole** — read how the ribbons or patch are organized between the poles.
> Which word the marble "wants" is itself a soft cue (Master *cutlines/poles*;
> Akro/Peltier/Marble King/Vitro *seams*; corkscrew *cutline*; Akro Moonie
> *pole*). For some types the diagnostic isn't the seam but a *named feature* —
> a corkscrew's ribbon start+finish, a Moonie's fisheye, an Acme Realer's interior
> "fire" — so chase that instead. When the seams won't decide it, **"maker
> uncertain" / "Akro or Master" is a valid answer** — no single tell (e.g.
> "eyelashes") is proof. The per-maker seam playbook is in **"Reading seams,
> cutlines & poles"** below; light it and shoot it per **Inspection shots**.

## When this applies

Any glass (or rarely clay/stone/steel) toy marble: loose, in jars, in lots, or
boxed. Includes "shooters" (larger marbles) and any item described as a swirl,
agate, cat's-eye, sulphide, onionskin, Lutz, or comic marble.

**Carve-outs / look-alikes that are NOT this module (or are filler):**
- *Agate* as a loose gemstone, cabochon, or jewelry stone — that's a mineral,
  not a marble. Only an agate *marble* (a sphere, toy) qualifies.
- Decorative glass spheres / paperweights / "witch balls" / nest eggs — larger
  art-glass orbs, not toy marbles (a paperweight has one pontil but is its own
  category).
- Steel ball bearings, clay "commies" sold as craft filler, modern craft/vase-
  filler glass gems (flat-backed) — filler, note and move on.
- **"Railroad logo" marbles** (printed railroad emblem) are **modern fantasy
  collectibles**, never issued by a railroad. *Genuine* railroad marbles are
  plain ~3/4″ greenish-clear spheres with bubbles + faint straw/tool lines, dug
  from rail ballast (B&O Cumberland Branch etc.) — undecorated; don't confuse
  the two.
- **Rock-tumbler stone spheres** — modern polished stones, sometimes passed as
  vintage; not glass toy marbles.

## Fast triage (the big-lot pass)

Sort the pile into **PULL** (look closer) vs **FILLER** (jar lot) in seconds:

**FILLER signals (the 90%) — leave in the bulk jar:**
- **Cat's-eye** with a simple injected color "vane/propeller" insert in clear
  glass — the classic 1950s+ mass-made style; common ones are pennies. (A few
  vintage/odd-vane cat's-eyes have minor value, but assume common.)
- Perfectly uniform, glossy, modern-looking color; bag-fresh; identical
  repeats by the hundred → modern Mexican/Chinese (Vacor/Mega, or rack-toy
  imports relabeled "Imperial"/etc.).
- **Orange-peel surface pitting + oily sheen** (under magnification) → modern
  Vacor; **frosted or iridescent** finishes never existed on vintage marbles.
- **White "hit marks"** (white impact spots rather than chips) + a deep U-shaped
  fold seam ("butt crack") + 3/4″+ size → modern **JABO** (see makers).
- Obvious chips/grinding on an otherwise common machine-made marble (damage
  guts common-marble value — see grading).
- Plain clearies, plain opaque "milkies," common single-color glass.

**PULL signals (worth a closer look):**
- **Two rough pontil marks** (rough/sheared spots at opposite poles) → **handmade**
  (cane-cut). Handmade is where the high-end pool lives — but pontils prove *method
  only*, NOT origin or age (see cardinal rule). Pull it, then judge origin/era
  separately.
- **One pontil + an embedded figure** (animal, person, number) → **sulphide**.
- **One pontil, ground/melted/creased** (no figure) → **hand-gathered /
  transitional** (early MFC, Navarre, Greiner) — pull; see pontil types.
- **Glittery gold/copper bands or sprinkles** in the glass → possible **Lutz**.
- **Mica flecks** (silvery metallic specks suspended in colored glass) → mica.
- A **printed picture/character or letters** on the surface → **comic/picture
  marble** (Peltier) — rare, pull immediately.
- Crisp **named machine-made patterns**: tight pole-to-pole spirals
  (**corkscrew**), opaque base with thin ribbons (Peltier **NLR**), white
  filaments + color in clear (**Popeye**), sharp non-bleeding colors
  (**Christensen Agate**).
- **Oxblood**: a dense, dark brownish-red opaque streak (prized on Akros, MFC and
  others — a value-adder when present). *(Specific oxblood sub-claims vary by
  source — treat as a "pull and verify," not a settled ID.)*
- **Size**: anything notably large for its type (≥ ~1 inch) — large examples of
  named types are disproportionately rare (e.g. *any Akro corkscrew over 1″ is
  extremely rare*). But a **jar of 3/4″–7/8″ colorful swirls is ~95% modern
  JABO/DAS**, not antique — size cuts both ways (see size-for-ID).
- Anything you simply can't place. **When in doubt, pull it.**

## Handmade vs machine-made (read it off the surface)

**First split — the decisive method read:**
- **Handmade** (cane-cut): **two rough pontil marks** at opposite poles.
  Single-gather types — **sulphides, paperweight-style — show ONE pontil**.
  No seams.
- **Hand-gathered / transitional** (turn of the century): a **single pontil** on
  one end with machine character on the other. The pontil style dates the
  technique, not the value — see the three pontil types below.
- **Machine-made** (American, ~1905–1950s "Golden Age" + later): **seams**
  (fold/cutoff/roller lines, e.g. Peltier's "Pelt line"), **no pontils**, high
  sphericity. The seam's *shape and name* vary by maker — see "Reading seams,
  cutlines & poles" below.
- ⚠ Do **not** date a handmade by how "smooth" the pontil is — the once-common
  pre-1880-smooth / post-1880-rough heuristic is **not reliable** (refuted
  against the specialist sources).
- ⚠ The two **patch-fold marks** on a machine-made (esp. Vacor) are easily
  MISREAD as pontils. A true pontil is *rough/granular* (or a clean ground facet
  / melted nipple / crease line); a fold mark is *smooth*. Texture is the tell.

**Pontil types on single-gather / transitional marbles (COLLECT.Guide / AMMM):**
1. **Ground / "faceted" pontil** — end ground smooth to a flat/faceted look.
   Earliest, e.g. **Greiner**. Gem-like facets, unmistakable in hand.
2. **Melted pontil** — reheated to a smoothed, rounded "nipple." Tied to the
   **J. H. Leighton** patented method; the **Navarre Glass Marble & Specialty
   Co.** made these in such volume that melted-pontil marbles are loosely called
   **"Navarre."**
3. **Creased pontil ("Transitional")** — a cut/shear line, often with a **drizzle
   tail** on the opposite pole and stretching in the pattern. Tied to **M. F.
   Christensen** and the **earliest Akro / Christensen Agate / Peltier** before
   full automation. Also seen on **Yasuda** (Japan, by 1929) and a Canadian
   "mystery marble." Often nearly invisible — a line, not a point.

## Handmade categories (the collectible ones)

- **Swirls / core swirls** — colored core or bands twisting through a (usually
  clear) base. Core sub-types: **latticinio** (a network of fine twisted
  threads — commonly white/yellow, rarer yellow-green), **solid core** (colored
  strands packed tight into one core), **divided core** (separate colored bands
  with clear gaps between them), **ribbon core** (flat ribbon[s] twisting
  through), and **coreless** (NO central band — strands/swirls only near the
  outer surface, often a green/blue/clear base).
- **Onionskins** — flecked/stretched colored "skin" over a clear/opaque core;
  panels common (e.g. 4-panel). End-of-day family.
- **End-of-day** — random multicolored flecks/cloud (made from leftover glass).
- **Lutz** — defined by **Lutz material** (ground copper / goldstone /
  aventurine) as glittering bands or sprinkles; NOT a maker (see money types).
- **Indians** — dark/black opaque base with colored surface bands.
- **Clambroths** — opaque base (often white milk glass) with many evenly-spaced
  thin colored lines pole-to-pole.
- **Micas** — colored glass with suspended silvery mica flakes.
- **Sulphides** — clear single-gather sphere with an embedded white figure.
- **Birdcage** — twisted latticino forming a cage-like pattern (single-pontil
  examples are notably rare).

## Reading seams, cutlines & poles (machine-made — per-maker)

The line where a machine-made marble's pattern closes goes by three loosely
interchangeable names — **seam**, **cutline**, **pole** — and *which* word a
collector uses is itself a soft maker/type cue. Most machine-mades have **two**
such lines at opposite ends. **Photograph BOTH ends:** the second line often
differs from the first, and seeing both frequently settles an ID. (This expands
on the method read in "Handmade vs machine-made" above and the "Pelt line" tell
in the roster — first-hand from the MC "Seams, cutlines and poles" tutorial by
Steph, moderator; per-maker named examples below.)

- **Master** — small **U- or V-shaped seams**, usually called **"cutlines"**; the
  points where ribbons meet top/bottom are sometimes called **"poles."** **Short
  seams + a V-shaped *second* seam** lean Master over Akro.
- **Peltier** — the lines are called **seams** (not cutlines). A busy pattern can
  *look* like it has three seams but has **two true seams** — hunt the real
  second one. (NLR ~1930±; later Rainbo span mid-1930s–1960s+.)
- **Marble King** — cutlines called **seams**; a **"patch-and-ribbon" pattern is a
  prime sign of Marble King.** ⚠ Spelling tell: MK = **"Rainbow"** (with a *w*);
  Peltier = **"Rainbo"** (no *w*) — same word, different maker. MK named combos
  from the tutorial: Rainbow Red, "blended," Bumble Bee (brown/yellow), Robin
  (light-blue base + red ribbons), Tri-Color Rainbow.
- **Akro** — mostly **seams**. On a **corkscrew**, the line is best called a
  **cutline**, and you should **highlight the ribbon's START *and* FINISH** in
  photos to confirm the corkscrew. On an **Akro Moonie** the cutline is a
  **pole**: the most identifiable Moonies show **"fisheyes" at the poles** —
  *earlier* Moonies are more translucent with an **open** fisheye (more
  desirable); *later* ones are more opaque with a nearly-closed fisheye.
- **Akro vs Master patch (a classic toss-up)** — Akro leans **longer seams** and
  **"eyelashes"** (eyelashes are *not* exclusive to Akro); Master leans **short /
  V-shaped seams**, often ~3/4″ shooter size. When it won't resolve, experts
  legitimately fence-sit: **"Akro or Master."** Don't force a maker.
- **Vitro** — **seams**; production spanned the 1930s–60s (Anacortes WA plant
  ~1990). Other features often matter more than the seam (Bull's Eye, Sweet Pea,
  Conqueror, Opal, Victory) — but **"when in doubt, start with the seams."**
- **Peltier patches** — rather than a seam, find the **end of the patch** (it can
  be wild/irregular). On a **translucent-white base**, get a shot showing the
  **"fire"/glow inside the glass to confirm an Acme Realer.** Pelt patches are
  famous for wild shapes, but some **Peerless Patches have squared-off edges that
  mimic Akro** — edge shape alone won't separate them.
- **Modern Asian ("Imperial")** — distributed in the US by Imperial Toy Co.;
  often very pretty, with **oxblood threads** common (here oxblood is *not* a
  rarity signal); near-infinite one-off color combos.

> These are recognition aids, not proof. Seam shape, "eyelashes," and even word
> choice shift the odds; they do **not** override the cardinal method≠origin≠era
> rule, and "maker uncertain" / "Akro or Master" remains a valid answer.

## Size as an ID clue (older American machine-made swirls)

Size won't name a maker alone, but it **eliminates candidates and shifts odds**
(COLLECT.Guide). Measure the diameter (calipers) before leaning on this.

- **21/32″–11/16″:** first think **Akro** & **Alley**; if it's a *swirl*, **Alley**
  is the strongest guess; if the pattern isn't Alley → **CAC / Ravenswood**.
  Champion rare here; **Heaton not at all**; Cairo possible but less common.
- **> 11/16″:** increasingly **Alley**. 11/16–3/4″ brings in **Champion "New Old
  Fashioneds."** At **7/8″+** it's almost entirely **Alley**, with rare
  Ravenswood/CAC.
- **< 5/8″:** later **Ravenswood** + **Champion** strongest; 9/16–19/32″ holds
  many of both (size alone insufficient).
- **Hover right at ~5/8″:** **Davis, Jackson, Heaton, Alox** tend to stay there.
- **≤ 1/2″ (peewee):** mostly **Alley** for swirls. Among non-swirls, Akro &
  Peltier made many peewees; Vitro & Marble King few (outside clearies).
- **Jar of 3/4″–7/8″ colorful swirls → ~95% modern JABO or DAS**, regardless of
  "estate"/"attic" origin story. This is the single most useful size heuristic
  for *avoiding* a false-antique call.
- ⚠ **Packaging ≠ maker:** Alox & Ravenswood bought Master shooters; Bogard
  bought Vitro/Marble King; Heaton bought Master/Vitro. A marble inside a branded
  bag is not proof that brand made it.

## The money types (named high-value items — the watch-list)

Pull and price-hunt these on sight:

- **Christensen Agate "Guineas"** — *holy-grail tier.* Transparent base
  (often clear/amber) with stretched, peppered multicolored flecks across the
  surface. Genuine Guineas reach **four figures** (a mint 19/32″ example
  carried a $750–$1,500 estimate / $1,400 bid at Block's). Heavily faked —
  verify hard.
- **Peltier National Line Rainbo (NLR)** — opaque base with **4–8 thin
  translucent ribbons** and **two seams**. Named by exact base/ribbon colors:
  - *Two-color:* Zebra (black/white), Bumblebee (black/yellow), Cub Scout
    (yellow/blue), Wasp (black/red), Tiger (black/orange), Chocolate Cow
    (black/brown).
  - *Tri-color (top):* **Christmas Tree** (white base, red+green), **Superman**
    (light-blue base, yellow+red), **Liberty** (white base, red+blue).
  - Named NLRs in mint run tens to several hundred dollars (Superman ~$125–$400+).
  - ⚠ A **Vacor "Serpent"** is occasionally mistaken for a Superman — check for
    orange-peel surface + modern color before calling NLR.
- **Peltier Comic / Picture marbles** — Peerless-patch base with a **fired-on
  black character transfer** under clear overglaze. Twelve King-Syndicate
  characters, ascending rarity: Emma, Koko, Bimbo, Smitty, Andy, Herbie,
  Skeezix, Annie, Sandy, Betty, Moon, Kayo — plus **Tom Mix** (red transfer,
  extremely rare). Any genuine comic is a serious pull.
- **Akro "Popeye" corkscrews** — **transparent clear base with opaque WHITE
  filaments** plus colored spirals (commonly red/yellow or green/yellow). The
  white filaments are *required* to be a Popeye. **Hybrids** (3+ colors) and
  **patch Popeyes** are rare and highly prized.
- **Akro corkscrews (general)** — two+ **non-intersecting** color spirals
  running pole-to-pole. Rarity rises with color count (**five-color "Specials"
  extremely rare**) and with **size — any corkscrew over 1″ is extremely rare.**
- **Lutz marbles** — any handmade with **Lutz material** (glittering ground-
  copper/goldstone bands or sprinkles). Subtypes: Banded, Onionskin, Ribbon,
  Indian, Mist/Solid-Core. **More Lutz on the core = more value** (most
  precisely true of onionskin cores).
- **Sulphides** — clear sphere with embedded figure; **colored or rare-figure
  (human, numbers, painted, doubles) sulphides** are the valuable ones; plain
  single-animal sulphides are more common.
- **End-of-Day Clouds & Onionskins** — handmade, transparent base with colored
  flecks. **Cloud** = flecks suspended *unstretched* (spotted); **Onionskin** =
  flecks *stretched* into a surface skin (converge at poles). Both are a real
  mid-to-high tier (clouds in ~5/8″ NM–Mint commonly $60–95+); a **dark/colored
  base** is rarer than the common white/yellow ground.
  ⚠ **Biggest trap:** a dark-base SPECKLED marble is far more often a **modern
  Vacor "Planet"/splatterpatch (~$1)** than an antique Cloud ($60–95). Before
  pricing a speckled marble as an antique Cloud, **run Google Lens** AND check
  the surface — Vacor's **orange-peel pitting + oily sheen** and the
  ~5/8″/.61″ size give it away. Antique Clouds have smoother period glass,
  genuine play-wear, and rough cane pontils (not patch-fold marks).
- **Clambroths, Indians, large onionskins/micas** in good grade — solid
  mid-to-high tier.

## Value drivers (priority order)

1. **Rarity** — the deep driver everything else proxies for. Handmade and
   short-run makers (Christensen Agate, Jackson, Davis) are scarce; later mass
   makers (Marble King, common Vitro/Akro patches, Vacor) are not.
2. **Maker + named type/pattern** — a *named* pattern (Guinea, NLR Superman,
   Popeye, a specific comic) is worth multiples of a generic swirl/patch.
3. **Color combination** — within a type, specific named/contrasty color sets
   command premiums (this is literally how NLRs are named and tiered).
4. **Condition / grade** — see below; interacts hard with #1.
5. **Size** — larger-than-typical examples of a named type are
   disproportionately rare and valuable (≥ ~1″ is a strong signal) — BUT see the
   size-for-ID JABO/DAS caveat: large + colorful + bulk usually means modern.

> ⚠ Do **not** use "made after 1932 = worthless" as a rule — that specific
> production-date heuristic was **refuted**. Date matters only through rarity,
> not as a hard cutoff. Likewise, several popular-guide "tells" are **weak/
> unreliable** and stay subordinate to the cardinal anti-bias rule:
> "small ~13–14 mm size" or a "central white bubble" → "German antique" (many
> German handmades are 5/8″+; bubbles occur across all eras); "more vibrant
> colors = antique" (false — Christensen Agate and modern Vacor are intensely
> vibrant); "smaller = less valuable" as a blanket rule (value tracks rarity,
> not raw size — named peewees and large examples both carry premiums); and
> "antique glass is harder / higher-quality than modern" (unsupported).

## Condition grading

**MCSA / standard scale (0–10, four named bands) — corroborated by COLLECT.Guide:**

| Grade | Range | Meaning |
|---|---|---|
| **Mint** | 9.0–10.0 | Original factory surface, no damage. Light storage/handling rubbing OK; *factory-origin* defects OK (popped air holes, melt/contact marks, annealing fractures). |
| **Near Mint** | 8.0–8.9 | Minor use: tiny/small hit marks, faint subsurface moons, pinprick chips, small flakes/bruises. If any noticeable damage, it's limited to one side; the other stays Mint. |
| **Good** | 7.0–7.9 | Clear use: more hit marks/moons/chips/flakes; core still visible. Deep damage usually one-sided. |
| **Collectible** | below 7.0 | Heavy use across the surface; core may be partly obscured. A placeholder until a better example is found. |

- Grading is **subjective**; dealers vary. Block's grades **conservatively**
  (treats Mint as 9.0–9.9, a true 10 as unattainable) and uses in-band
  plus/minus. **Under a 10× loupe every marble shows marks — nothing grades a
  true 10.**
- **Damage vocabulary** (post-use): **moon** (subsurface ring fracture, no
  glass loss), **bruise** (subsurface discoloration, no loss), **chip** (deep
  angular break with loss), **flake** (shallow surface loss), **fleabite**
  (tiny nick), **hit mark** (white impact spot — common on JABO).
- **Condition cuts value far harder on machine-made than handmade.** Per MCSA,
  *even a small chip cuts a machine-made marble's value to less than half* —
  because they're common. A **rare handmade** holds much of its value even
  damaged (collectors forgive damage on hard-to-find pieces). So: damage on a
  common marble → filler; damage on a rare handmade → still pull.

## Authentication & fakes

- **Buffed vs polished (high-value skill — both get NO grade).** A reworked
  surface is a **red flag to detect**, not a value-add:
  - **Buffed** — a *thin* abrasive layer removes haze/tiny pits to fake grade.
    Pontils on a handmade **usually survive**; the marble feels **slippery**,
    deeper damage (chips, subsurface moons) **remains**, and the original
    as-made grain (wooden-cup / roller marks) is **gone**.
  - **Polished** — removes *much more* glass. A handmade **loses its pontils
    entirely**, and grinding the top/bottom to keep it round **opens the pontil
    areas**. On a machine-made, polishing can **expose subsurface air bubbles**
    and **shift the color** (heat). Opened/absent pontils on an otherwise old-
    looking marble = polished.
- **Reproductions / modern art-glass.** Contemporary makers (e.g. **Brian
  Graham**, working on the original Miller/Peltier roller) produce hand-gathered
  marbles with genuine pontils that can fool the eye; modern handmades often have
  very saturated colors, large size, and sometimes a maker signature/date cane.
  **Two rough pontils do NOT prove antique.** Separate antique from modern on
  glass quality, color idiom, genuine play-wear (vs artificial distressing),
  size, and design; when they don't clearly add up, stay at **"handmade — era
  undetermined."**
- **Fantasy packaging.** Beware bags with maker headers stuffed with later
  marbles: **Alox "Army/Navy/Air Force"** fantasy bags (often modern Marble
  Kings or Champion swirls), and modern marbles **fraudulently repackaged in
  Champion / Alox headers.** The bag is not the marble.
- **Vacor passed as vintage.** The classic modern-as-old scam. Orange-peel
  surface, modern colors, and frosted/iridescent finishes (which never existed
  vintage) give it away. Conversely, some early Mega Marble designs
  (Michelangelo, retired runs) are now genuinely collectible — judge on the
  specific design, not "modern = worthless."
- **Faked rarities.** Guineas especially are faked. Demand crisp type-specific
  structure, sensible size, period wear, and ideally provenance; a too-clean,
  too-cheap "Guinea/Superman/Popeye" is reproduction until proven otherwise.
- **Honesty guard:** an unconfirmed rare type is `[BEST-CASE]` with the exact
  resolution step, never a stated ID. A wrong "Guinea" call is the most
  expensive mistake in this category in both directions.

## Inspection shots

When the photos can't settle identity or condition, request (via
`needs_followup_photo`) the SPECIFIC shot, not "more photos":

- **Pole macro (both ends)** — to read pontils (handmade: two rough; sulphide/
  transitional: one — note ground/melted/creased) vs seams (machine-made). The
  decisive method shot.
- **Raking-light surface macro** — to grade (chips/moons/flakes) and detect
  buffing/grinding flats, opened pontils, and Vacor orange-peel.
- **Backlit / transmitted-light shot** — for swirls, NLR ribbons, oxblood,
  Lutz/aventurine glitter, mica, and to see a sulphide figure clearly.
- ⚠ **Light it from the SIDE, never flat/overhead.** Flat overhead light makes a
  **transparent base read as opaque/black** and hides the type. Side/backlight
  before judging transparency or type.
- **UV (blacklight) shot** — many WV swirls and esp. **JABO** (Fenton scrap) and
  **Jackson** (Vitrolite) **fluoresce**; UV reaction is a real maker/era clue.
- **In-hand with calipers/coin** — diameter is a value driver AND feeds the
  size-for-ID table; note inches/mm.
- **Close-up of any printed image/letters** — for a suspected comic/picture
  marble.

**Camera craft (first-hand — MC "photo tips" tutorial).** *How* to light/back a
shot, complementing the shot list above and the shoot convention in
[`../inventory/marble-photo-protocol.md`](../inventory/marble-photo-protocol.md)
(those frame counts change only by mutual agreement — these tips refine the
frames, not their number):

- **Show both seams/poles**, not one face — the second end often differs and is
  what clinches a machine-made ID (see the per-maker seam section).
- **Gray, smooth background** (Steph shoots on a gray t-shirt): gray helps simple
  cameras auto-balance color; *smooth* keeps autofocus on the marble, not the
  cloth — if the weave/stitches come out sharp, the background is too textured.
- **Kill reflections:** natural light throws reflections that bury detail — try a
  **flash**, or shoot **transparent marbles UNDERWATER** (dramatic for
  cloudy-surface old German handmades; brush surface bubbles off first).
- **Bright-yellow marbles** need different white-balance/color settings than
  duller ones — expect to re-shoot to get the color right.
- **Target the diagnostic feature:** corkscrew → ribbon **start + finish**; Acme
  Realer (translucent-white Pelt patch) → the interior **"fire"/glow**; opal /
  opalescent Vitro → the **opalescent flash**.

## Good / Better / Best — mandatory output + poll when uncertain

Every marble (and marble lot) is reported as a **GOOD / BETTER / BEST** ladder on
type — three live tiers, never a single verdict. Lead with the bull case; the
floor is *optimistic*, not pessimistic; make the downgrade EARN it with a
concrete observable; grade a clean surface **Mint**, not "NM pending". This is
the speculative-upward mandate applied to marbles.

- **GOOD (floor):** the conservative-but-not-pessimistic read — e.g. "vintage
  machine-made swirl, Mint, sells as a matched set", **not** "filler".
- **BETTER:** the named-maker / better-grade step up, with the cue that supports it.
- **BEST:** the named money-type / trophy read if the tells align — stays
  `[BEST-CASE]` + verify, but is STATED, not buried.

**Read everything you can off the photos yourself — only poll the user for what a
photo can't show.** Pattern, seam count, ribbons-vs-patch, colour, base
transparency from a backlit frame: YOUR call. When the tier genuinely can't be
settled AND value swings on it, poll with the SPECIFIC question(s) below
(interactive) or log to `needs_followup_photo` + `NEEDS_REVIEW.md` (headless) —
never silently default to the low tier.

**Decision question bank (each tied to which tier it moves):**
1. **Provenance / age** of the lot → sets the age dial (`lib/marble_prior.py`).
2. **Size by caliper** (mm/in) → >5/8" adds; ¾"+ and 1"+ jump tiers (size is a
   top multiplier even on a common type).
3. **Pole check** → rough/grainy pontils (handmade → BEST) vs smooth seams (machine).
4. **Backlit** → glows translucent (common) vs dense opaque brick-red (oxblood →
   up) vs thin translucent ribbons + two seams (Peltier NLR → up); **and is the
   base SEE-THROUGH** (clear → Akro/Vitro veneer) **or opaque** (→ Marble King)?
5. **Surface (raking + loupe)** → orange-peel + oily sheen (modern Vacor → down)
   vs smooth period glass; any chip/moon (grade down) vs clean (Mint → up).
6. **UV / blacklight** → does it fluoresce? (WV swirl / JABO Fenton-scrap /
   Jackson Vitrolite maker-era clue).
7. **Colour character** → crisp, intense, non-bleeding (Christensen Agate → BEST)
   vs soft/bleeding.
8. **Count / match** → how many of this exact type, all mint? (matched mint set =
   premium unit, not bulk).

**Ribbons vs patch — read it off the photo, do NOT ask:** broad colour zones/caps
+ a band where they meet at the seam = **patch / patch-and-ribbon** (Marble King &
common patches); **4–8 thin parallel pinstripes** pole-to-pole + two seams =
**Peltier NLR** (the $$ upgrade). This single read is often the $1-vs-$$ fork —
make it yourself, then use the backlit/seam questions only to confirm.

## Output hooks (maps onto IDENTIFY fields)

- **Brand** → the maker when a named type/seam/pattern confirms it (Christensen
  Agate, Akro, Peltier…); `[BEST-CASE]` + bracket for thin-coverage / WV-swirl
  makers or unconfirmed rarities; **handmade** marbles are **Unbranded/maker-
  unknown** — call them "handmade" (cane-cut) and only add "German" when origin
  evidence supports it, not from pontils alone. For modern bulk, name the family
  (Vacor/Mega, JABO, Imperial-style import) when the tells fit.
- **Type** → the named type/pattern (e.g. "NLR Superman", "Akro Popeye
  corkscrew", "4-panel onionskin", "sulphide — dog", "WV opaque swirl"). This is
  the highest-leverage field for marbles.
- **Era** → earn it separately from method. Handmade: antique (~1850–1920) vs
  modern/contemporary studio is often unresolvable from photos → default
  **"handmade — era undetermined" [BEST-CASE]** unless glass/wear/signature
  decide it. Machine: Golden Age ~1905–1950s; WV swirl boom ~1930s–50s; modern
  1980s+. Never upgrade to "antique German" on pontils alone.
- **Condition** → MCSA grade + band (e.g. "Near Mint 8.5 — two small subsurface
  moons; no chips") per the rubric above; flag buffed/polished as **no grade**.
- **Distinguishing marks** → record the **value tier** ($ / $$ / $$$ / $$$$ /
  🏆) here so PRICE/CURATE know which to comp-hunt vs jar; plus size (diameter),
  pontil/seam read, UV reaction, and Lutz/mica/oxblood presence.
- **needs_followup_photo** → the inspection shot above (usually the pole macro,
  or a UV shot for a suspected WV swirl/JABO).
